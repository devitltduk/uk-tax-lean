# The model printed as English: a spike on ITA 2007 s35

This spike asks whether the Lean model can be printed as readable English and as
statute-style text, deterministically and without an LLM, in a way that guarantees
the printed text matches the code. It covers the personal allowance and its taper
(ITA 2007 s35) and the rate bands the allowance feeds (the limits in s10, the main
rates in s6).

- [`s35.md`](s35.md) is the plain-English version.
- [`s35-statute.md`](s35-statute.md) is the statute-style version.

Both files are generated by `lake env lean --run Prose.lean` (after `lake
build`) and must not be edited by hand. CI regenerates them and fails if they differ from the committed copies.

## How it works

`UkTax/Rules/Core.lean` is a small rules language made of blocks shaped like
drafting:

| Block | Meaning (`eval`) | Plain English | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| figure | a sum from `TaxParams`, e.g. `P.pa` | "£12,570" | "£12,570" |
| derived figure | a figure the Act works out from others, e.g. the higher rate limit; its listed parts are proved to add up to it | "it is twice the personal allowance (£12,570) plus …" | "equal to— (a) twice the amount specified in …, plus (b) …" |
| input | a fact about the taxpayer, e.g. adjusted net income | "your adjusted net income" | "adjusted net income", with a definition subsection |
| threshold test | `threshold < input` | "If your adjusted net income is more than £100,000" | "For an individual whose adjusted net income exceeds £100,000" |
| taper | `a − excess × per ÷ every`, exact | "take off half of the amount by which it is more than £100,000 (£1 for every £2 over …)" | "is reduced by one-half of the excess" (or "£1 for every £2 of the excess") |
| not less than nil | `max 0 a` | "If that leaves less than £0, use £0." | "Subsection (2) does not reduce the allowance below nil." |
| round up to £1 | ceiling to a multiple of £1 | "round it up to the next whole pound" | s35(3)'s own wording |
| "subject to" | (structure only) | none | "(1) Subject to subsections (2) to (4), …" |
| rate bands | Σ rate × the part of income in each band | a table of bands and rates | "(a) at the basic rate, on so much of it as does not exceed the basic rate limit, (b) …" |

Money inside the rules is held in 1/10 000ths of a penny, so halving an odd
number of pence is exact. Every block except a figure or an input wraps exactly
one amount. Any amount is therefore a starting figure followed by a list of
adjustments, and the statute printer turns that list into subsections (1), (2),
(3)… in the way a section of an Act states an amount and then adjusts it.

`UkTax/Rules/S35.lean` writes s35 and the s10/s6 bands in these blocks. The
figures are read from a `TaxParams`, so the text quotes the 2026-27 figures and
changes when they change: with `pa := £13,000` and `paLimit := £110,000`, both
documents and their worked examples change to match. It also proves the
following, with no `sorry`. A `#guard_msgs` axiom check fails the build if that
ever changes.

- `personalAllowanceRule_eval`: the rule equals the model's `personalAllowanceFor P ani`.
- `mainRates_eval`: the bands equal the model's `bandTax P.nsRates rg x`.

Both hold **for every parameter set**, with no `TaxParams.Valid` needed, and for
every input. The band theorem assumes `Regime.relief = 0`, meaning no Gift Aid or
relief-at-source pension contributions raising the limits. Each proof is about 10
lines, mostly `omega`.

### What the guarantee covers, and what it doesn't

- **Guaranteed by proof and by construction.** The figures quoted come from the
  same functions that `eval` uses. The worked examples in `s35.md` are computed by
  `eval`, so they are the model's own values. The structure of the text (which
  adjustments, in which order, applied to what) is printed from the same value the
  theorems are about. If someone changes the rule in a way that changes its
  meaning, the proof breaks. If someone changes it in a way that changes its
  wording, the CI diff breaks.
- **Checked once by reading, not by proof.** Whether each block's fixed wording
  means what its `eval` does. There are nine blocks, so this is a one-off review
  of the table above, not a per-section review. The plain taper step needed care
  here: "£1 for every £2" could be read as whole £2 steps, but the law and the
  model halve exactly. The plain text therefore says "worked out exactly rather
  than in whole £2 steps".
- **Not covered at all.** The plain-English glosses of "adjusted net income" and
  "taxable income" are explanatory free text. The gloss for adjusted net income
  simplifies s58 (it leaves out the add-back of trade union and police
  organisation relief).

## Assessment

**How close it reads.** For s35 the generated text is very close: subsections
(2), (4) and (5) are word for word or nearly so. That is partly by design, because
the wording of each block was chosen to echo the Act's drafting. It also works
because s35 really is a chain of "an amount, then adjustments to it", which is
exactly the shape the printer produces. The bands in s10 read naturally too, in
the "(a) … (b) … and (c)" style of a charge.

**What differs, and why.**

- **Conditions the model does not have.** The Act's "who makes a claim" and the
  s56 residence condition are missing, as are "for a tax year" and Treasury
  orders. The model assumes the allowance is claimed and the person is resident,
  so the generator has nothing to print. This is the general limit: the printer
  can only say what the code computes, and statute says much more than that.
- **Where the code is more explicit than the Act.** Subsection (3), "not reduced
  below nil", has no counterpart in the Act. The Act leaves it implicit that an
  allowance cannot be negative, but the code has to say it, so the text does too.
  The "subject to" opener is an ordinary drafting device, but s35 does not use it.
- **Where the model differed in structure from the Act (now fixed).** The Act
  defines the higher rate limit as twice the personal allowance plus the income
  limit (s10(5A)). When this spike was written, `TaxParams` held it as a separate
  figure, so the generated s10(3) just stated £125,140 and the proof held for
  values that broke s10(5A). The model now follows the Act: `TaxParams.hrl` is
  worked out from the allowance and the income limit, so no parameter set can
  break the link, and the generated s10(3) prints the derivation. The parts it
  lists are proved to add up to the limit the model uses
  (`higherRateLimitFigure_derived`). The one thing still left out is s10(5B), the
  Treasury order that states the resulting figure each year from 2028-29.
- **What is missing.** Definitions, cross-references and the "subject to" lists.
  The text has no s10(4) list of overriding sections (Scottish, savings, dividend
  rates), no s10(6) gift aid and pension increases (the proof assumes relief is
  nil), no indexation, and s6(2) states the rates instead of deferring to
  Parliament's annual resolution. "Taxable income" is a term the generator
  invented, where the Act relies on the step order in s23. These are drafting
  conventions about where things live in the statute book, not about what is
  computed, so a calculation model cannot produce them.

**Would a non-specialist understand the plain-English version?** Yes, for the
personal allowance. The four steps and the worked examples (including £100,001,
which shows the rounding) are clear. The one hard idea is "adjusted net income".
The gloss makes it usable, but a reader with unusual reliefs would still need
GOV.UK. The band table is clear, but it is easy to misread "the first £37,700" as
applying to total income rather than income after the allowance. The gloss and the
examples deal with that, but a full calculation page would need the step that
links the two sections (income minus allowance, then bands), and that step is not
covered here. An honest "no" for the statute version: it is legible only to
someone used to reading Acts, as the real one is.

**Is it worth doing for the rest of the model?** Partly. For rules with the same
shape (an amount with adjustments, a taper, a schedule of bands), the cost is low
and the result is good. That covers the HICBC, the Universal Credit taper and work
allowance, employee and self-employed NICs, SDLT/LBTT/LTT bands and the student
loan thresholds. Each needs a handful of lines in the blocks and a short `omega`
proof, and gives a plain explanation with worked examples that cannot drift from
the code. That is genuinely valuable for the web app and the write-up. It is not
worth pursuing as a generator of statute for the whole model:

- The model's hardest parts are algorithms, not drafted rules: the s25(2) search
  for the cheapest way to deduct allowances, savings and dividends "treated as the
  highest part" with nil-rate bands that still use up the band, and the Scottish
  limits raised by relief. Writing them in blocks would need ordering and
  allocation blocks the Act does not have in that form. Proving equivalence there
  is real work, not 10 lines of `omega`.
- The statute layout shows the idea works, but most of what makes statute text
  statute (definitions in other sections, cross-references, claims, conditions,
  indexation) is not in the computation. The better target is the plain-English
  version, plus a link to the real section for the legal text.

Recommendation: keep the blocks and add rules one programme at a time where a
plain-English explanation is wanted, starting with the HICBC and the UC taper,
which share the taper block. Drop the statute layout unless someone asks for it.
CLML/Akoma Ntoso output was not attempted. With this structure it would be a
third renderer over the same subsection list, but it would only be worth doing
alongside real cross-references.
