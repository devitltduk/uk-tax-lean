# A synthetic population for 2026-27

> **Every figure on this page is a synthetic estimate.** The households are invented, not
> real ones; they are weighted to published totals from open statistics. Only the
> calculation for each household (`uktax`, the proved Lean model) is proved; the
> population itself (which households exist, their incomes, their weights) is ordinary
> statistical code in `population/`, and is not. The figures are rough: see
> [How rough](#how-rough).

Generated by `uv run --project population population/run.py docs` (30,000 synthetic benefit units, seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Where a figure is given as a
range, it is the lowest and highest over the five seeds; a single figure is the same in all five
at the rounding shown. Do not edit by hand.

## What this is and isn't

- **Synthetic, not real households.** No survey or administrative record is used: no Family
  Resources Survey, no Survey of Personal Incomes microdata, nothing from the UK Data Service.
  The households are drawn by rules (`population/model.py`) from published tables, and then
  weighted so that their totals match those tables.
- **Open data only.** Every source is published under the Open Government Licence v3.0 and
  downloaded without an account or key (list below). `population/fetch.py` downloads them and
  extracts the figures into `population/margins.json`, with each file's SHA-256.
- **Rough.** The published totals fix how many households of each kind there are and how
  many taxpayers in each income range, but not how incomes combine within a household. That
  comes from assumptions (below), and it matters most for the cliffs.
- **Proved per household, not as a population.** Each household's income tax, NICs, HICBC,
  Child Benefit, Universal Credit and Carer's Allowance come from `uktax`. The weights,
  totals and counts are statistics, with no proof.
- **The unit is the benefit unit**: a single adult or a couple, with their dependent
  children. A household of several adults (grown-up children, sharers) is several units here.
  There are about 40,459k units against 29,003k households (ONS, 2025).

## How the population is built

1. **Draw** 30,000 benefit units: single adults, couples, lone parents and couples with 1, 2 or
   3+ children, people over 66 (pensioners), in the proportions of the ONS family counts.
   Country and London by population. Children's ages from Child Benefit's single-year counts.
2. **Incomes.** A working adult draws an income range from HMRC's 2026-27 projection (Table 2.5),
   or an income below the personal allowance; within the range, from a curve with the range's
   published mean income. The income is split into pay or self-employment, property, interest
   and dividends by that range's shares (HMRC Tables 3.6 and 3.7, 2023-24); workers aged 55 to 65
   may also draw a private pension. People over 66 get a
   State Pension near the full new rate and the rest from their own range draw. Employees paid
   £10,000 or more pay the automatic-enrolment minimum (5 % of qualifying earnings) by net pay.
   The ranges above £100,000 are drawn more often and weighted down, so each has enough units.
3. **Housing, caring, health.** Tenure by income and age; private rents around ONS's average
   (London's in London), with a Local Housing Allowance below it; social rents around the
   Regulator's average. Some low earners are carers; some adults not in work have limited
   capability for work. Families register for Child Benefit, and high earners opt out, by
   assumption; Universal Credit is claimed by 87 % of those entitled.
4. **Compute** every adult with `uktax` (`.lake/build/bin/uktax`): partners first, then the
   head, whose Universal Credit claim counts the partner's net earnings and any Carer's Allowance.
5. **Weight** the units by minimum-entropy calibration: weights as close as possible to the
   design weights such that the weighted totals equal the published targets (next section).
   Taxpayer counts, Universal Credit, Child Benefit and Carer's Allowance counts use the
   engine's results, so the weights are fitted after the calculation.

Money totals (tax, NICs, benefit spending) are **not** targets. They are the check.

What the gaps mean:

- **Income tax** is a few per cent high. The income in each HMRC range matches HMRC's within
  a per cent or two (it is drawn to), so the excess is in the deductions: only the
  automatic-enrolment minimum pension contribution is modelled, not larger contributions, salary
  sacrifice, relief at source, Gift Aid, losses, the Marriage Allowance or the married
  couple's allowance. Receipts also differ from liabilities by timing.
- **NICs** are well above 2025-26 receipts. Pay is somewhat high (pay is the residual of each
  range's income after other sources), salary sacrifice is not modelled, and the employer
  figure ignores the Employment Allowance (up to £10,500 per employer) and the lag of receipts
  after the April 2025 rise. Employer NICs are on this population's pay only.
- **Universal Credit** is high. Every claimant with the health element is given the protected
  (higher) rate; rents and the 87 % take-up are assumptions; capital, the minimum income
  floor, the disabled child elements and childcare costs are not modelled; and UC households are
  only those whose adults have pay or a trade alone.
- **Child Benefit**: the engine pays 53 weeks in 2026-27 (53 Mondays); the forecast is a
  spending figure. Families are fitted to August 2025 counts.

## Changing the figures

The same population, run again with other figures (the parameter set of PR #33 and #34, passed
to `uktax` as `params`). Cost is to the Exchequer: the rise in households' net income (income
less income tax, employee NICs and the HICBC, plus Child Benefit, Universal Credit and Carer's
Allowance). No behaviour changes.

| Change | Cost (synthetic estimate; negative raises money) | Better off | Worse off | Mean gain of those better off |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Personal allowance £12,570 → £13,570 (basic rate limit unchanged) | £9.1bn | 31,515k to 31,682k | 0k | £286 to £289 |
| Basic rate 20 % → 21 % on non-savings income (UK rates; Scottish rates unchanged) | £-7.2bn | 0k | 29,570k to 29,667k | £0 |
| Personal allowance taper starts at £125,000 instead of £100,000 | £2.7bn to £2.8bn | 1,095k to 1,150k | 0k | £2,396 to £2,530 |
| Universal Credit taper 55 % → 50 % | £2.0bn to £2.1bn | 2,692k to 2,767k | 0k | £735 to £766 |

Checks against HMRC's ready reckoner (June 2025; receipts, so the first year is lower than a
full year's liabilities):

- personal allowance +£1,000: ten times HMRC's £100 figure is £8.1bn (2026-27) and £10.5bn (2027-28), against £9.1bn here. A little comes back through
  Universal Credit, whose award falls by 55 % of the rise in a claimant's net earnings.
- basic rate +1p: HMRC £6.9bn (2026-27) and £8.2bn (2027-28), against £7.2bn raised here. Here the savings rate
  is unchanged, and Universal Credit makes up part of what its claimants lose.

By hand: a basic rate taxpayer with income over £13,570 gains £200 from a £1,000 higher
allowance, a higher rate taxpayer £400 (the higher rate threshold moves up with it), and
someone in the taper gains up to £400; with about 40,800k taxpayers that is roughly
£8bn to £10bn. Starting the taper at £125,000 helps only adjusted net incomes between £100,000
and £150,140 (where the allowance now runs out); HMRC has 1,190k taxpayers between £100,000 and £150,000. The
most, at £125,000, is £12,500 more allowance at 40 %, £5,000; the average is about half that.

## How rough

Three kinds of uncertainty, largest first:

1. **Assumptions where no open table exists.** How incomes pair within couples (independent
   here), who opts out of Child Benefit, who claims Universal Credit, who is a carer or has
   limited capability for work, tenure and rents, and how investment income splits between
   working-age people and pensioners. The weights fix the totals, not these joint patterns.
   Counts of small groups near a cliff can be out by a factor of two; national money totals
   by a few per cent to a few tens of per cent (see the gaps above).
2. **The published figures themselves.** HMRC's 2026-27 income figures are projections from
   the 2023-24 Survey of Personal Incomes; ONS family counts are Labour Force Survey estimates
   with 95 % intervals of about ±1 % to ±10 %; the income splits by source are 2023-24's; the
   Child Benefit counts are August 2025's.
3. **Sampling of the synthetic units.** The ranges over five seeds above show it: small for
   totals, larger for the cliff counts.

## Known weaknesses, and what would improve them most

- **Couples' incomes are drawn independently.** This matters most for the £100,000 and HICBC
  cliffs. An open table of couples' joint incomes (for example ONS's published household
  income distributions by family type) would give a target for it.
- **Pension Credit, Housing Benefit, council tax and its reductions, disability benefits,
  Tax-Free Childcare and the funded hours are not in the totals**, though the engine computes
  them. Adding Pension Credit and PIP flags would cover most of the remaining spending.
- **Deductions**: only the automatic-enrolment minimum. HMRC's private pension statistics
  (open) would let contributions above the minimum and relief at source be fitted.
- **Universal Credit**: no capital, no minimum income floor, no disabled child elements, no
  childcare costs; every health element at the protected rate. DWP's published element counts
  could be added as targets one by one.
- **Regions**: only the four countries and London, which is all the engine needs except for
  rents.

lake build uktax                                   # the engine
python3 population/fetch.py                        # optional: download the sources again
